Prepositions of Place

Master the Art of Expressing Location in English

IN

General Use: Used for enclosed spaces, countries, cities, and larger areas.

Standard Examples:

  • The keys are in the drawer.
  • She lives in London.
  • The children are playing in the garden.
  • There's a surprise in the box.
Exception: Use "at" for specific points or addresses, even within cities.

Exception Examples:

  • Meet me at 42 Baker Street (specific address).
  • She's waiting at the bus stop (specific point).
  • I'll see you at the corner of Main Street.
  • The party is at my house (specific location as a point).

ON

General Use: Used for surfaces, floors, and when something is touching something else.

Standard Examples:

  • The book is on the table.
  • There's a picture on the wall.
  • She lives on the second floor.
  • The cat is sitting on the roof.
Exception: Use "in" for vehicles when you're inside them as a passenger.

Exception Examples:

  • I left my phone in the car (inside the vehicle).
  • She's sitting in the taxi.
  • The passengers are in the train.
  • He's sleeping in the bus.

AT

General Use: Used for specific points, locations, and addresses.

Standard Examples:

  • I'm waiting at the entrance.
  • She's at the office.
  • Meet me at the restaurant.
  • They arrived at the airport.
Exception: Use "in" when referring to being inside the building or space itself.

Exception Examples:

  • I'm working in the office (inside the space).
  • She's sitting in the restaurant (inside).
  • The students are in the classroom.
  • We're having lunch in the cafeteria.

UNDER

General Use: Used when something is directly below something else.

Standard Examples:

  • The cat is hiding under the bed.
  • Put your bag under the table.
  • There's a tunnel under the river.
  • The toys are under the couch.
Exception: Use "below" when something is at a lower level but not directly underneath.

Exception Examples:

  • The valley lies below the mountain.
  • The temperature is below zero.
  • Her apartment is below mine (lower floor).
  • The submarine traveled below the surface.

ABOVE

General Use: Used when something is at a higher level but not necessarily directly over.

Standard Examples:

  • The birds are flying above the trees.
  • Hang the mirror above the fireplace.
  • The temperature is above average.
  • His apartment is above the shop.
Exception: Use "over" when something is directly on top or covering something.

Exception Examples:

  • Put a blanket over the child.
  • The bridge goes over the river.
  • She wore a coat over her dress.
  • There's a roof over our heads.

BETWEEN

General Use: Used when something is in the middle of two things.

Standard Examples:

  • The shop is between the bank and the post office.
  • Sit between your mother and father.
  • The ball rolled between the two cars.
  • There's a fence between our houses.
Exception: Use "among" when something is surrounded by more than two things.

Exception Examples:

  • She found her keys among the papers.
  • The house is hidden among the trees.
  • He felt comfortable among his friends.
  • The treasure was buried among the rocks.

BEHIND

General Use: Used when something is at the back of something else.

Standard Examples:

  • The garden is behind the house.
  • Stand behind the yellow line.
  • She hid behind the door.
  • The parking lot is behind the building.
Exception: Use "in back of" in American English as an alternative, especially for vehicles.

Exception Examples:

  • The luggage is in back of the car (American English).
  • Sit in back of me in the theater.
  • The supplies are in back of the store.
  • There's more space in back of the van.

NEXT TO / BESIDE

General Use: Used when something is at the side of something else.

Standard Examples:

  • The lamp is next to the bed.
  • She sat beside her best friend.
  • The library is next to the museum.
  • Place the chair beside the window.
Exception: Use "by" for proximity without specifying which side.

Exception Examples:

  • Come and sit by me.
  • The house by the lake is beautiful.
  • Keep your phone by your side.
  • There's a café by the station.
